Bully stick vs gullet stick: the same brands, side by side

Bully sticks and gullet sticks are both beef chews, but brands make them from different parts of the animal. Seven brands in our directory sell both, so this page puts each brand's bully stick next to its gullet or esophagus chew and quotes what the pages say about ingredients, origin, calories and chewer type. Where a page is silent, we say so. We don't pick a winner; your vet can help you choose for your dog.

Last checked 2026-10-29 · page updated 2026-10-30

Before you give any chew: supervise your dog while it chews, choose a size and hardness that suits your dog (follow the brand's size guidance), take away small or broken pieces, keep fresh water available, and ask your vet if a chew is right for your dog, especially for puppies, seniors, dogs with dental work or dogs on special diets. Nibbables does not rate safety. Each card shows what the brand itself says.

Gullet stick vs bully stick: what's the difference?

They're made from different parts of the animal. All seven brands' bully sticks list pizzle (Redbarn: “Beef Pizzle.”; Raw Paws: “Single-ingredient: Bull Pizzle!”). Their gullet chews list gullet or esophagus instead: Raw Paws “Single-ingredient: Beef Gullet!”, Nature Gnaws “Ingredients: Beef Gullet”, Sancho & Lola's Closet “Ingredients: Dehydrated Beef Weasand (Esophagus)”. Jack's Premium explains that “Weasand is the muscle meat that surrounds the esophagus.” Not every esophagus chew is esophagus alone: Redbarn's lists “Collagen (from Beef Hide), Beef Esophagus.”

What lasts longer than a bully stick?

We found no study that measures how long gullet sticks or bully sticks last. Several brands describe their gullet chews as the softer option: Jack&Pup calls its gullet sticks “a great alternative to bully sticks as they're softer and easier to chew”, Raw Paws lists “Chew Hardness: Light.”, and Best Bully Sticks says its braided gullet sticks “offer more of a challenge than a standard gullet stick, but they're not as tough as braided bully”. How long any chew lasts depends on its size and on how your dog chews.

How many calories are in a gullet stick?

Only some pages say. Best Bully Sticks lists “Calories Each 114” for its 6-inch braided gullet sticks and “Calories Each 38” for its 5-6 inch jerky gullet strips, and Nature Gnaws gives “About 45 calories per chew” for its 4-5 inch gullet chews. For bully sticks, Nature Gnaws gives “About 70 calories per chew” for its 6-inch stick and Jack's Premium says “Bully sticks contain about 15 kcal/inch for a thin stick.” The Raw Paws, Jack&Pup, Sancho & Lola's Closet, Jack's Premium and Redbarn gullet or esophagus pages we checked don't state a calorie figure. Chews differ in size and thickness, so these numbers can't be lined up directly; our bully stick calories guide covers the published study that measured bully sticks.

Gullet sticks for puppies: what do the brands say?

Nature Gnaws says its 4-5 inch gullet chews are “Ideal for moderate chewers and teething puppies and small breeds.” Jack&Pup's feeding instructions say “Not suitable for puppies under 3 months old.”, while the same page's general FAQ mentions “puppies 6 months and older”. Sancho & Lola's Closet describes its weasand strips as a “LIGHT CHEW: Ideal for young dogs, seniors and those with dental issues.” Ask your vet before giving any chew to a puppy.

Where are gullet sticks made?

Some pages name a country and most don't. Raw Paws lists “Origin: Colombia.” for its gullet sticks and “Origin: Brazil.” for its esophagus strips. Redbarn lists “Country of Origin: Paraguay, Brazil” for its collagen-wrapped esophagus stick. Jack's Premium says “Sourced & Made in USA: We never source our meat from overseas.” for its weasand. The Best Bully Sticks, Jack&Pup, Nature Gnaws and Sancho & Lola's Closet gullet pages we checked don't name a country; Best Bully Sticks says “Our beef gullet is sourced from trusted free-range cattle farmers around the world.”

The same seven brands, side by side

Raw Paws, Best Bully Sticks, Jack&Pup, Nature Gnaws, Sancho & Lola's Closet, Jack's Premium and Redbarn each sell both a bully stick and a gullet or esophagus chew, so the table puts each brand's two pages next to each other. Origins can differ within one brand: Raw Paws lists “Origin: Turkey.” for its standard bully sticks and “Origin: Colombia.” for its gullet sticks.

What veterinary sources say about gullet chews

We didn't find a veterinary source that discusses gullet or esophagus chews by name. Today's Veterinary Practice lists “Other body parts (e.g., ears, trachea, lung, hooves, antlers, horns, bully sticks, tendons, masseter muscles, skin, hides)” under “Animal-derived products that have undergone minimal processing”; esophagus isn't named in that list. The AKC notes that “different animal parts are marketed as edible dog chews.” The ASPCA recommends that people “read and follow label directions explicitly, and choose products designed for the species, size and chewing habits of their pets”. Ask your vet which chew suits your dog.

What each brand says about supervision

Most of these gullet pages ask you to watch your dog. Raw Paws says “always supervise your pet when giving any chew.”, Best Bully Sticks says “Please supervise your dog when giving any chew or treat”, Jack&Pup says “Always supervise your dog while chewing and provide fresh water.”, Nature Gnaws says “Always supervise your dog while they are chewing and provide plenty of fresh water.” and Redbarn says “Always monitor your pet while rewarding with a chew”. We found no supervision line on the Sancho & Lola's Closet or Jack's Premium weasand pages we checked.
