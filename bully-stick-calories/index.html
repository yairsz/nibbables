Bully stick calories: what a vet study measured and what brands state

Bully sticks add calories to a dog's day, and in a veterinary survey half of owners underestimated how many. This page quotes the one peer-reviewed analysis of bully stick calories we found (Canadian Veterinary Journal, 2013), veterinary nutritionists at Tufts on how treats fit into a dog's daily calories, and the calorie and per-day wording on each bully stick product page in our directory. Nibbables doesn't calculate or recommend amounts; your vet can tell you how many calories your dog needs.

Last checked 2026-10-23

Before you give any chew: supervise your dog while it chews, choose a size and hardness that suits your dog (follow the brand's size guidance), take away small or broken pieces, keep fresh water available, and ask your vet if a chew is right for your dog, especially for puppies, seniors, dogs with dental work or dogs on special diets. How many calories are in a bully stick?

It depends on the stick's length, thickness and weight. In a 2013 Canadian Veterinary Journal study, five 5- to 7-inch bully sticks bought in the US were analysed by a commercial lab; the authors reported “a mean total caloric density of 88 kcal/treat (range: 45 to 133 kcal/treat)”. Brand pages that give a per-stick figure run from Bow Wow Labs' “58-87 calories per stick” for its 6-inch midsize stick to “244-302 calories per stick” for its 12-inch jumbo; Nature Gnaws says “About 70 calories per chew” for its small 6-inch stick (see the table above).

How many calories per inch is a bully stick?

The same study reported: “Mean caloric density was 15 kcal/inch”, with a range of 9 to 22 kcal per inch and 2.96 to 3.07 kcal per gram. A 2015 correction changed only the per-centimetre conversions (to 5.9 kcal/cm, range 3.5 to 8.7 kcal/cm); the per-inch figures stand. Lisa M. Freeman, DVM, PhD, one of the authors, summarised it on Tufts' Petfoodology blog: “an average 6-inch bully stick is nearly 100 calories!”

How many calories are in a 12-inch bully stick?

The study's authors wrote that “bully sticks could provide between 54 to 132 kcal for a 6″ bully stick and 108 to 264 for a 12″ bully stick.” In its owner survey, “Calories in a 12″ bully stick were underestimated by 50% of respondents.” Among brand pages, Best Bully Sticks lists “Calories Each 86” for its 12-inch standard odor-free stick, and Bow Wow Labs gives 116 to 302 calories per 12-inch stick depending on thickness.

How many bully sticks a day can a dog have?

Nibbables doesn't set a number. Campfire Treats says of its 8-10 inch sticks, “we recommend giving your dog up to one bully stick per day”, and of its 4-6 inch sticks, “between one and three of these bully sticks per day”. Best Bully Sticks says “bully sticks should not make up more than 10% of your dog's daily caloric intake.” Deborah E. Linder, DVM, writing for Tufts Petfoodology, says “your pet should only receive 10% of their daily calories from treats so you don’t unbalance their diet!” Ask your vet how that applies to your dog.

Can dogs have bully sticks every day?

The study gives an example rather than a rule: “If the mean for a 6″ bully stick were used (i.e., 90 kcal), 1 bully stick daily would be equivalent to 9% of the daily calorie requirements for a 50-pound (23-kg) dog and 30% of the daily calorie requirements for a 10-pound (4.5-kg) dog”. For comparison, Tufts Petfoodology says a 10 lb dog's treat maximum would be “only 30 calories for treats” and an average 75 lb dog's “a maximum of 140 calories for treats.” Whether a daily bully stick fits your dog's diet is a question for your vet.

Why per-stick calories vary so much

The study's authors found the sticks were similar by weight but not by size: “The number of calories measured in the 5 bully stick samples was similar on a weight basis. However, the length and width of bully sticks varies widely so total calories in an individual treat will vary accordingly.”

That matches how brands present their numbers. Bow Wow Labs lists a separate calorie range for each length and thickness. Campfire Treats, GREAT DOG CO. and Pupford give calories only per kilogram (Campfire also per ounce), so you'd need the stick's weight to work out a per-stick figure. Mighty Paw's page gives “~160 kcal per stick” but doesn't say which of its stick sizes that refers to. Redbarn's page lists “Calories: N/A”.

Other findings in the same study

The study also tested 26 bully sticks for bacteria. In the authors' words: “Of 26 bully sticks that were tested for bacterial contamination 1 (4%) was contaminated with Clostridium difficile, 1 was contaminated with methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA), and 1 with a tetracycline resistant Escherichia coli.” Dr. Freeman's Petfoodology summary says the results emphasize “the importance of washing your hands after touching these treats, as you should with any raw meat or raw meat diets.”

About the sources

The study is Freeman LM, Janecko N, Weese JS, “Nutritional and microbial analysis of bully sticks and survey of opinions about pet treats”, Canadian Veterinary Journal 2013;54(1):50–54 (Tufts Cummings School of Veterinary Medicine and the Ontario Veterinary College), with a 2015 correction. It analysed five sticks for calories, so it's a small sample from one point in time. Brand calorie figures are each brand's own statement, quoted as published; Nibbables hasn't measured any product.
