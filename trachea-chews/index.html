Beef trachea chews are hollow, ridged tubes that brands describe as the windpipe of a cow; Jack's Premium says "Trachea is not a bone; it is the windpipe made up of cartilage and muscle." Brands differ on where the beef comes from, which dog sizes and puppy ages they name, and how many calories they list. Each card quotes the brand's own product page, and anything a page doesn't say is marked Not stated.

Beef trachea is the same part of the cow, whoever sells it. But Bully Bunches lists 99 calories an ounce, while Campfire and Jack's Premium list 159. That's about 60 percent more.

Most people assume an American brand means American beef. Bully Bunches says packaged in the USA, product of Brazil or Turkey. Campfire says sourced and made in the USA. And Nature Gnaws' sourcing line names Europe, the Americas, and Nepal.

Got a puppy? Jack and Pup's trachea page says it's not suitable for puppies under three months. But the FAQ on that same page says six months and older. If your puppy is in between, ask the brand and your vet.

And don't expect it to last all afternoon. Jack's Premium says trachea is softer and hollow, and most dogs go through one in about 30 minutes.
