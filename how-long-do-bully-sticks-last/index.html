How long do bully sticks last? Shelf life, storage and the last piece, quoted

People ask three different things about how long a bully stick lasts: how long an unopened bag keeps, how to store one your dog has started, and when to take the last piece away. Most brand product pages don't answer the first two, and the brands that do don't always agree. Below, each of the 18 bully stick product pages in our directory is quoted side by side, followed by what guides from Redbarn and three other bully stick sellers, and the FDA, say. Nibbables hasn't tested storage methods or timed any chews; follow the date and instructions on your bag, and ask your vet if you're unsure.

Last checked 2026-10-26 · page updated 2026-10-30

Before you give any chew: supervise your dog while it chews, choose a size and hardness that suits your dog (follow the brand's size guidance), take away small or broken pieces, keep fresh water available, and ask your vet if a chew is right for your dog, especially for puppies, seniors, dogs with dental work or dogs on special diets. Nibbables does not rate safety. Each card shows what the brand itself says.

Do bully sticks go bad? Here's what eighteen product pages and four seller guides say.

Only four of the eighteen product pages state a shelf life. Redbarn and Pawstruck say thirty-six months. Bully Bunches says one to two years. Jack and Pup says months.

Natural Farm's guide says to throw one out if it has fuzzy white, green or black spots, or a sour or ammonia-like smell.

Storing a chewed stick? Brands disagree. Redbarn says air dry it and don't refrigerate. Natural Farm and Bully Sticks Direct say refrigerate it.

And the last piece? Redbarn says take it away once it can be swallowed whole, about two and a half to three inches in most cases.

Every brand's exact wording is at nibbables dot com. Check the date on your bag, follow the brand you bought, and ask your vet if you're unsure.

Do bully sticks go bad?

Yes, according to the brands that address it. Natural Farm's guide says “Unopened bully sticks last 2-3 years when stored in a cool, dry place below 80°F.” and lists signs to throw one out, including “Mold. White, green, or black fuzzy spots anywhere on the surface.” and “If a stick develops a sour or ammonia-like smell after chewing, it's gone bad. Discard it.” Natural Farm also says: “Always check the best-by date on the bag.”

How long can you keep a bully stick?

Only 4 of the 18 bully stick product pages in our directory state a shelf life: Pawstruck and Redbarn each say “Shelf Life: 36 months”, Bully Bunches says “Shelf life of 1-2 years.”, and Jack&Pup says only “Properly stored chews will maintain their quality and freshness for months.” The other 14 pages don't say. Two seller guides give ranges for unopened sticks: Natural Farm says 2-3 years, and Bully Sticks Direct says “~18–24 months typical; up to 2–3 years under ideal conditions.” The date printed on your bag is the brand's own answer for that batch.

Should you refrigerate a bully stick after your dog chews it?

Brands disagree. Redbarn's guide says to “let the bully stick air dry”, then “store the bully stick in a cool, dry place where your pet can’t reach, like a high cabinet or pantry shelf”, and adds: “We do not recommend refrigeration as the moisture could potentially attract bacteria.” Natural Farm says the opposite for a chewed stick: “Once your dog starts chewing one, seal it in an airtight bag, refrigerate, and use within a week.” Bully Sticks Direct says: “Dry and refrigerate chewed sticks, and discard after a couple of days.” Natural Farm and Bully Sticks Direct both sell bully sticks. Follow the brand you bought, and ask your vet if unsure.

How long should a bully stick last a dog?

None of the 18 product pages we list says how many minutes or days one stick should last. Brutus & Barnaby, a bully stick seller, writes: “Most dogs should chew a bully stick for about 10 to 30 minutes at a time, depending on their size, chewing style, experience level, and stomach sensitivity” and “If your dog is new to bully sticks, start with a short session: 10 to 15 minutes”. Nibbables hasn't timed any chews.

When to take a bully stick away?

Redbarn's guide says: “Take the bully stick away and discard it once it can be swallowed whole (about 2.5 - 3 inches in most cases).” Brutus & Barnaby says: “When the stick gets small enough to swallow, it is time to take it away”. On the product pages, 11 of 18 say something about the last or broken pieces (see the table): for example Jack&Pup says “Remove the chew if it becomes small enough to swallow or breaks into pieces.” and Raw Paws Pet Food says “If this chew splinters, develops sharp edges or breaks into pieces throw it away immediately.”

Bully stick holders for the last piece

Redbarn's guide mentions holders: “Some pet owners also use bully stick holders to ensure that their dogs are not able to consume the last portion of a bully stick.” Bow Wow Labs sells one and describes it on its bully stick page (quoted in the table). Nibbables hasn't tested any holder.

What the FDA says about storing dry pet food and treats

The FDA's pet food handling tips aren't specific to bully sticks, but they cover dry pet food: “Store dry pet food in a cool and dry place. The temperature should be less than 80 F.” and “Store dry pet food in its original bag and keep the top of the bag tightly folded down.” The FDA also says to “wash your hands for at least 20 seconds with soap and hot water” before and after handling pet food and treats.
