Lamb and goat horn dog chews: what each brand says about origin, size and fat

Nibbables editorial team. Every claim on this page is quoted from the brand's own page or the source linked next to it; we don't test, rate or review products. Page updated 2026-11-06. How we verify.

At a glanceAll products

Lamb and goat horns are single-ingredient keratin chews sold as a long-lasting option for dogs that chew hard. Brands describe them as made of keratin (not bone), sometimes with marrow inside. If you're looking up a lamb horn dog chew or a goat horn for dogs, we compare only what each brand's own page states: the ingredient, where the horn comes from, size and weight ranges, fat and calorie figures where listed, and the supervision steps each brand names. Stated origins differ (Turkey; free-range Icelandic lamb; Jack's Premium does not name a country on its goat horn page). Ask your vet whether a hard horn chew suits your dog's teeth and chewing style.

4 products · every claim is quoted from the brand's own page and linked · no prices (they change; check the brand) · compare all in one table

Before you give any chew: supervise your dog while it chews, choose a size and hardness that suits your dog (follow the brand's size guidance), take away small or broken pieces, keep fresh water available, and ask your vet if a chew is right for your dog, especially for puppies, seniors, dogs with dental work or dogs on special diets.
