Bully stick vs yak chew: what the brands and the AKC say

Nibbables editorial team. Every claim on this page is quoted from the brand's own page or the named source linked beside it; we don't test, rate or review products. Page updated 2026-11-01. How we verify.

Bully sticks and yak chews are two of the most common single-ingredient-style chews, and they're very different things: one is dried beef pizzle, the other is hardened cheese. This page puts 14 products from our directory side by side, quoting each brand's own page on ingredients, where it's made and where it's sourced, and adds what the AKC and a veterinary journal say about each type. We don't pick a winner; your vet can help you choose for your dog.

Sources last checked 2026-10-24

Before you give any chew: supervise your dog while it chews, choose a size and hardness that suits your dog (follow the brand's size guidance), take away small or broken pieces, keep fresh water available, and ask your vet if a chew is right for your dog, especially for puppies, seniors, dogs with dental work or dogs on special diets. Bully stick vs yak chew: what's the difference?

They're made from different things. Redbarn describes a bully stick as “a single-ingredient dog chew made from 100% beef pizzle”, and the bully stick pages in the table list beef pizzle (or just beef) as the ingredient. Yak chews are hard cheese: Raw Paws says “we boil yak milk and then add lime juice and salt, which curdles the milk and forms cheese”, and the AKC describes them as “hard blocks of dried cheese”. Four of the five yak chew pages say they're made in Nepal or the Himalayas; the bully stick pages that say where they're made name the USA, countries in South America, New Zealand or Turkey.

Which lasts longer, a bully stick or a yak chew?

We found no study that measured it. Brands on both sides call their chews long-lasting: Redbarn says its bully sticks “create a long-lasting chew dogs love”, and Pawstruck says of its yak chew “still longer chew time than a bully stick!”, which is the brand's own claim. The AKC says yak chews “are hard enough to be long-lasting”. How long any chew lasts depends on its size and on how your dog chews.

Do yak chews or bully sticks have more calories?

The few brands that state calories mostly give them per kilogram, and the figures are close. Campfire Treats states “Calorie Content: 3,320 kcal/kg; 94 kcal/oz” for its bully sticks. For yak chews, Raw Paws states “3,168 kcal/kg., 90 kcal/oz.” and Pupford “Metabolizable Energy: 3,074 kcal/kg” (“About 329 kcal per chew.” for its medium chew). A per-chew figure depends on the chew's weight; see our bully stick calories guide for the published study.

What the AKC says about each

On bully sticks, the AKC says they “are easily digestible” and adds that “power chewers might bite straight through, producing large pieces that pose a choking risk, or swallow the entire stick whole.” It suggests a bully stick holder for those dogs and says: “When your dog has chewed the stick down to a nub, take it away before they can swallow what’s left.”

On yak chews, it says “The lactose content is low, so dogs generally digest them well”, and also “although these are cheese, they are quite hard and could lead to broken teeth.” Its advice: “only give your dog a block large enough that they can’t eat whole.”

How a veterinary journal classes them

Today's Veterinary Practice (two board-certified veterinary nutritionists) lists bully sticks among “Other body parts” in the group of “Animal-derived products that have undergone minimal processing”. The article doesn't mention yak or cheese chews, so we can't say how it would class them.

Where they come from

Of the five yak chews in the table, Mighty Paw says “Handcrafted in Nepal”, Pupford “sourced and made in the Nepal area of the Himalayan mountain range”, Raw Paws “Origin: Nepal.” and Pawstruck “made in the Himalayan mountains by local craftsmen”; Best Bully Sticks doesn't say where its yak chew is made. The bully sticks vary more: Campfire Treats says “Sourced and made in the USA”, while Redbarn and Pawstruck list several countries in South America plus New Zealand and Turkey, and Raw Paws says “Origin: Turkey.”
