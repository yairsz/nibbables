Pig ear dog chews: what each brand says about origin, fat and sizes

Nibbables editorial team. Every claim on this page is quoted from the brand's own page or the source linked next to it; we don't test, rate or review products. Page updated 2026-11-05. How we verify.

At a glanceAll products

Pig ears are dried, sometimes smoked, pork ears sold whole, halved or sliced. If you're asking whether pig ears are good for dogs, the brand pages below make their own claims, and we leave those on their pages rather than repeat them as fact. What we compare is what each page states: the ingredient, where the pork comes from and where the chew is made, the fat and calorie figures, and which dogs it names. The stated origins differ: one page says sourced and made in the USA, one says pork from Brazil, and Redbarn lists Canada, Mexico and South Korea for its natural ears. The stated minimum fat runs from 5% to 9% on the pages that list it. Anything a page doesn't say is marked Not stated. Ask your vet whether a pig ear suits your dog.

Before you give any chew: supervise your dog while it chews, choose a size and hardness that suits your dog (follow the brand's size guidance), take away small or broken pieces, keep fresh water available, and ask your vet if a chew is right for your dog, especially for puppies, seniors, dogs with dental work or dogs on special diets.
